# Paper revision: evidence, contribution and Gemma integration

## Editorial recommendation

**Make the paper about exact completed repository observations, and use the 0.13 transport experiment as its single empirical study.** This is a stronger research position than either a feature inventory or a claim that binary encoding improves coding agents. The existing evidence supports a measured runtime resource; it does not support model-level gains.

Recommended title: **Suffice: Exact Repository Observations for Developer Agents**.

Recommended subtitle: **Versioned artifacts, explicit completion and a controlled transport study**.

Working thesis: *An optimization to a developer-agent tool should be evaluated while preserving the same declared observation: the captured version, selected row order, paths, complete bodies and successful completion. Suffice makes that contract executable and inspectable. Its same-binary transport comparison shows substantial local service improvements while retaining an empty-output counterexample and explicit limits on agent-level interpretation.*

The old packet is read-only evidence, identified by SHA-256 `bfd32da30fe9ed6ee86c199e4cc453985bd5e7a9e33db3b08259d4b55d474e53`. Its careful qualifications are a strength. Its main weaknesses are the loosely specified agent use case, broad implementation narrative, one nearly all-output predicate, and a main empirical result superseded by newer work. These are weaknesses in the research argument, not evidence of false reporting.

The old checklist recorded a 3,000-word maximum and related systems/resource papers within scope. Current rule compliance is being rechecked separately; this critique does not certify eligibility or silently turn a Gemma experiment into a mandatory competition rule. The substantive relevance gap remains even where an agent experiment is optional.

## Three defensible contributions

1. **An explicit observation and completion contract.** For immutable generation G and request q, define `O(G,q) = [(row_i, path_i, body_i)]`. A successful result preserves that ordered sequence and reconciles its completed row/body counts. Exactness is relative to the selected generation and request; it does not imply repository-wide transactionality during capture.
2. **An executable systems resource with failure evidence.** The resource implements generation ownership, semantic admission, named retained-state budgets, full-output framing and sticky stream failure. Parser, writer and actual-file tests examine different layers. The result is supported by code and tests, not a formal verification claim.
3. **A controlled complete-service observation.** The 0.13 comparison changes response transport within the same binary/client and compares identical full outputs. Four nonempty scenarios improve locally; empty output has mixed timing and more response traffic. This boundary is part of the contribution.

Do not claim a new theory of caching, a novel serialization primitive, automatic dependency discovery, arbitrary build reuse, a proof-carrying runtime, global optimality, or an advanced coding agent already evaluated. The paper should cite SWE-agent for agent-computer interface motivation; it should not transfer that paper's model-performance evidence to Suffice. [S1]

## Exact empirical story for the revised paper

The main experiment has 100 fresh processes, 4,840 complete-output checks, 23,480 returned rows and 419,571,140 repeated compared body bytes. It uses one preserved 103-file corpus partitioned into twelve logical projects. Ten matched process blocks per scenario are the timing observations. The corpus size is 1,460,207 bytes; the larger comparison total is repeated delivery, not unique data volume.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Reuse | 2.13808 | 2.13298 | 10/10 | 49.58869% |
| Pressure | 1.79944 | 1.77344 | 10/10 | 49.57385% |
| Mixed queries | 1.90233 | 1.89121 | 10/10 | 49.45453% |

Use fewer decimals in the paper; retain full values in the ledger/notebook. Ratios above one favor Binary. Convert g to an equivalent reduction as `1 - 1/g`, not `g - 1`. Do not headline the empty geometric mean as a consistent benefit. Its median favors JSONL and only four blocks favor Binary.

The primary service measure spans process creation, admissions, runtime work, complete transfer and Python materialization, statistics and shutdown, with explicitly timed oracle/audit intervals subtracted. Keep raw-wall comparisons as a sensitivity result. Avoid causal decomposition from overlapping phase clocks. Both arms use Capture and the same 16 MiB retained Worker limit, and finish every matched pair with the same native state/counters. These are limits on named components, not RSS. No model, agent task or native Windows run occurred.

Primary evidence: `suffice_v0_13/results/transport_v0_13/{suite.json,RUN.json,SUMMARY.json}`, all 100 process records, frozen measurement source, `REPORT.md`, and `docs/transport_experiment_v0_13.md`. The claim ledger records exact identities. The previous paper's 0.11 measurements should be omitted from the main tables; qualitative historical motivation is sufficient.

## Proposed Gemma controller: concrete and deliberately limited

**Revision boundary:** a subsequent, separate completed-read bridge now exists under `kaggle_revision_v2/integration`. Its native receipt passed 114 checks across four processes, including 12 complete observations, six cross-transport equality checks and two injected nonempty-prefix failures. This reviewer inspected its binding/completion logic and verified all seven recorded input identities; no blocking issue was found under its exclusive serialized-client contract. The bridge receives already-parsed arguments and exposes host-approved generation-local row ranges, not path search. No Gemma parser/template, tokenization or inference was executed. Its validation is separate from the frozen 100-process transport experiment; the full editing lifecycle and task evaluation below remain proposals.

Google's current Gemma 4 documentation supports function declarations through the processor's chat template and separates proposed model calls from application execution. Gemma 4 has its own tool-call delimiters and stopping lifecycle. Use a pinned instruction-tuned checkpoint, processor/template and supported inference stack rather than a handwritten substitute prompt. No exact inference-library API is claimed tested here. [S2–S4]

### Read lifecycle

1. Capture a quiescent task workspace and call existing `OPEN(project, catalog_root, expected_revision)`. Record the returned disk generation G and catalog-local revision R.
2. Under the existing serialized caller discipline, call `PIN(alias, project)`. Its current response contains the revision, **not** a generation field; check that revision equals R and bind the controller's alias to the earlier G/R record.
3. Expose a narrow model tool, for example `read_artifacts(generation, paths, max_bytes)`. This is a **new adapter API**, not an existing native command. Resolve paths through a verified generation-specific path-to-row index; a first implementation can obtain that index from a bounded complete enumeration and charge the cost. The runtime currently filters four integer features and global row ranges; it is not a path-search or semantic-retrieval service.
4. Dispatch bounded reads with existing `RUNPIN` (`catalog.run(alias, ..., pinned=True)`). Validate the returned generation/revision against G/R. `RUN` itself has no expected-generation parameter, and a row number must never be reused across generations merely because a path looks similar.
5. Stage rows until the client receives a valid success terminal. Only then commit a deterministic tool response to model history. Prefix callbacks are not transactional; a later error must not leave a partial result presented as complete.
6. `UNPIN` when the observation phase ends. An intentionally retained historical alias must be labeled historical; it must not silently satisfy a request for the latest edited workspace.

The adapter should render exactly the same model-visible text and token IDs for JSONL and Binary. Wire metadata, timing and cache-hit diagnostics belong in the experiment log, not varying tool messages. If files exceed the model's context budget, return an explicit budget error or offer a separately specified excerpt tool with visible ranges/completeness. Do not silently drop rows, replace full bodies with hashes or claim the previous full-body experiment establishes excerpt performance. Nontext bytes need a declared representation/exclusion rule shared across baselines.

### Editing and execution lifecycle

`apply_patch` and `run_tests` are separate controller tools; Suffice does not implement them. Before a mutation, verify the workspace base expected by the controller, apply the operation in the task's isolated workspace, wait for completion, capture a coherent new generation, and publish with the expected native revision. Advance the model-visible generation only after success. The current producer can recompress all admitted bodies after a change; per-edit capture and full admission must be charged in an agent evaluation.

Allowlisted tool dispatch, schema/range validation and workspace confinement are enforceable controller properties. They do not establish that a model is immune to malicious instructions embedded in repository text. Tool-result content remains data, with parser/template boundary cases tested explicitly. No model-generated text should be evaluated as arbitrary controller code.

A minimum run manifest records checkpoint/revision, tokenizer and template digests, inference-library revision, dtype/quantization, decoding settings/seeds, thought-mode setting, task/base commit, tool schemas, context truncation policy, all budgets, and runtime/evaluator identities. A minimum trace records call ID, requested generation, native alias/sequence, exact arguments, response descriptor/completion state, rendered message digest, token counts and phase timing. Do not publish hidden model reasoning merely to make tool execution auditable; model-call parameters and external tool observations suffice for these invariants.

## Evaluation that can reject the integration hypothesis

### Stage A: adapter conformance, before model inference

Compare proposed adapter responses against a direct-filesystem oracle on the same immutable manifest. Cases include same reads, absent paths, rename/insert/delete, old pins after publication, stale revisions, invalid UTF-8, all-byte bodies, zero-byte files, oversized responses, partial terminal failure, duplicate call IDs, context-budget rejection and source text containing tool delimiters. Acceptance: zero incorrect successful observations, zero stale-generation substitutions, identical rendered text/token IDs across transports, and no partial output committed as success. Deadlines require a separate controller supervisor; the present client does not supply bounded request/QUIT completion.

### Stage B: replay real tool traces, without claiming model improvement

Collect a small fixed set of actual Gemma-issued repository read sequences, retain permitted task inputs and freeze the traces before choosing policy. Replay identical operations and generation transitions through: (B0) ordinary filesystem reads of the same immutable snapshots with no result cache; (B1) Suffice JSONL; (B2) Suffice Binary. The plain baseline may compute the requested view efficiently; it must not be forced through needless native-like serialization. Use the same model-facing view contract and output oracle. Report capture/admission separately and end-to-end, observed useful bytes, tool-call service, native/client resources and failures. The completed 0.13 trace study is prior backend evidence, not a substitute for these real-tool traces.

Primary transport contrast: B1 versus B2 with all policies fixed. Separate ablations can disable complete-result retention, release Workers, or compare Capture/Recompute **within one new frozen build**. Generation pinning/completion removal belongs only in controlled failure experiments, not an “equivalent faster” production arm. A common JSON/base64 or standard framed-library arm can test how much advantage comes from choosing hex as the baseline; SFC1 superiority to other competent binary protocols is not presently established.

### Stage C: paired Gemma task episodes

Use one frozen agent loop/model for both transports. Start with a plumbing pilot on a declared public issue list, then a separately frozen evaluation set and seeds; do not tune on the held-out results. SWE-bench's official harness grades patches in repository-specific containers. Its result cache can be keyed by run and instance identity, so give each distinct configuration/seed/patch evaluation a distinct run ID. [S5]

Report single-attempt issue resolution, total elapsed time, input/output tokens, tool time, model time, capture/update time, peak native/client/model resource measurements and failures. Keep all attempted tasks in denominators; timeouts are outcomes. Pair by task and seed, balance execution order, and account for repository/task clustering instead of treating calls as independent trials.

Two contracts answer different questions. Under equal model-token/step budgets and identical tool messages, transport should preserve capability; divergent messages or unexpected decisions first require a parity investigation. Under a separately prespecified wall-time budget, faster tools might permit more useful steps, but that is a new task-level hypothesis. Inference can dominate total cost, so backend ratios cannot predict an end-to-end agent speedup without measuring the tool fraction.

Promotion gates: first, zero conformance mismatches; second, a reproducible retained-record audit; third, a prespecified latency/resource improvement on held-out traces; fourth, an adequately powered task-success comparison with a justified noninferiority margin or superiority criterion. A tiny pilot can establish plumbing, not noninferiority. If uncertainty crosses the chosen margin, the conclusion is inconclusive. No proposed threshold or future task count should be formatted as an executed result.

## Space and presentation priorities

A roughly 2,600-word paper can allocate about 160 words to the abstract, 220 to motivation, 450 to the contract/mechanisms, 380 to methods, 340 to results, 250 to the proposed Gemma connection, 220 to limitations, 180 to related work, 190 to references and the remainder to title/headings/conclusion. Keep an all-inclusive margin under the competition maximum.

Use one compact mechanism figure that visibly distinguishes implemented runtime from proposed agent controller, and one paired-results figure that shows every block including Empty. Caption the figures with the observational unit and completion/agent boundary. A complete pin/edit/deadline evaluation program belongs in supplementary engineering notes, while the paper states its most important missing gate clearly.

## Completed-read bridge review addendum

The actual bridge uses `STATE` to recheck host-approved generation/revision/rows, verifies the `PIN` revision, privately collects `RUNPIN` output, then verifies terminal generation/revision/retirement and the exact constant-true row range. Only a successful complete observation becomes canonical UTF-8/base64 JSON. The reviewed cases produce equal rendered strings across transports without timing, cache or sequence fields. The rendered-byte cap is checked after materialization and is not a peak-allocation bound. Scope lifecycle and latest-worktree correctness remain host responsibilities. The bridge does not add request deadlines, edits, path traversal, semantic search or model execution.
